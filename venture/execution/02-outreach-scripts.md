# 02 — Outreach Scripts

Principles (from simulated buyers, UNVALIDATED): never say "replace your broker"; lead with a
specific regulation and a deadline; name the licensed broker who signs; zero-risk offer; no ERP
write access; no "AI" in the first sentence.

Placeholders: `{First}`, `{Company}`, `{HTS_chapter}`, `{origin}`, `{trigger}`, `{Broker_Name}`
(our licensed broker), `{Founder}`.

## Sequence A — Industrial distributor / manufacturer CFO (A1, A2)

**Email 1 (day 0) — subject: 232 derivatives at {Company}: 0% or 25%?**

> {First} — since April 6, a derivative with more than 15% foreign metal by weight pays 25% of its
> *full* customs value; at 15% or less it pays nothing. Import records suggest {Company} brings in
> {HTS_chapter} goods from {origin}, which usually means a few hundred SKUs sit near that line.
>
> We run a 14-day scan of your last 18 months of entries (read-only ACE report access, no change
> to your broker) and return three numbers: duty still recoverable before its deadline, 232 exposure
> by SKU, and refund rights you should be preserving while the Section 301 case is decided.
> {Broker_Name}, a licensed customs broker, signs every finding.
>
> It's free for our first ten design partners. Worth 20 minutes next week?
>
> {Founder}

**Email 2 (day 3) — subject: the deadline nobody tracks**

> One more thing worth knowing: in July the trade court ordered IEEPA refunds on finally liquidated
> entries only for importers that had sued. Everyone else is waiting. The same question now applies
> to the 10–12.5% Section 301 duties you've paid since July 24. We track every entry's liquidation
> date and protest window so that decision is never made by default. Happy to show you yours.

**Email 3 (day 8) — subject: close the loop?**

> If duty sits with someone else at {Company}, who should I speak to? If now's wrong, I'll send a
> two-page note on the 232 cliff and leave it there.

**LinkedIn (connection note, ≤300 chars):**
> {First} — we help distributors prove which SKUs fall under the 232 15% metal line and track
> refund deadlines while 301 is litigated. Licensed-broker signed. Free scan for 10 design partners.

**Cold-call opener (30 seconds):**
> "Hi {First}, {Founder} from Keelson — this is a cold call; can I have thirty seconds and you tell
> me if it's worth more? … Since April the 232 derivative rule charges 25% of full value above 15%
> metal by weight, and most distributors we speak to can't show the evidence line by line. We do a
> free 14-day scan of your entries — your broker stays — and hand you the dollars and the
> deadlines. Would it be crazy to look at your last 18 months?"

## Sequence B — Consumer-durables VP Ops (A3)

**Subject: January 1 furniture/cabinet step-up — which SKUs?**
> Section 232 rates on upholstered furniture, cabinets and vanities are scheduled to rise from 25%
> on January 1, 2027. We'll map your SKUs to the new rates, show which are exposed, and find any duty
> you can still recover — in 14 days, read-only, without touching your forwarder arrangement.

## Sequence C — Litigation-aware importer / GC (A4)

**Subject: 301 forced-labour refunds — your liquidation calendar**
> The CIT heard the 301 forced-labour challenge on September 30. If the tariffs fall, the IEEPA
> precedent suggests finally liquidated entries may be refunded only to importers who acted in time.
> We maintain an entry-level calendar of liquidation dates and protest windows, and hand filing-ready
> packages to your trade counsel. No change to counsel; we make their work faster.

## Partner pitch — independent customs broker

> You file the entries; your clients now ask about 232 evidence, refund deadlines and 301 litigation
> you don't have hours to chase. Keelson gives you a white-label scan and ledger for your clients:
> we do the evidence work, you keep the entries and the relationship, and you earn 20% of our
> subscription revenue for 24 months on clients you introduce. We never file for your clients.

## Partner pitch — CPA / fractional CFO

> Your manufacturing clients' duty line tripled and their accrual is a guess. We give you a free
> exposure scan for any client, a month-end duty accrual report, and a referral fee. It makes your
> close and your advisory work better; it takes nothing from your engagement.

## Objection handling

| Objection | Response |
|---|---|
| "My broker handles this." | "Good — keep them. Brokers are paid per entry, so re-checking last year's entries isn't their job. We send your broker corrected instructions." |
| "Won't this trigger CBP attention?" | "Corrections filed before liquidation are routine. If we find an underpayment, counsel reviews it and you decide — prior disclosure caps penalties. Not knowing doesn't reduce exposure." |
| "Is this AI?" | "AI reads the documents; a licensed customs broker makes and signs every determination." |
| "We don't have time." | "It's read-only ACE access and one item-master export. Under two hours of your team's time." |
| "25%/15% is too much." | "You pay only on cash CBP returns, capped, and only for in-window recoveries — nothing on the ledger findings you act on yourself." |
