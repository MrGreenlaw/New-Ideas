## Tariff durability
- Section 122 struck by CIT (May), expired Jul 24 (B).
- Section 301 forced-labour tariffs: 10/12.5% on 60 economies, ~99.4% of imports, ~$954B import value; Tax Foundation projects $612B net 2026–35 (A/B). **Challenged by importers + 25 state AGs (filed Aug 3); CIT three-judge panel heard representative case Sept 30, 2026** (B). Plausible 6–12 month path to a loss.
- Section 232 stickier and expanding (B).
- Tax Foundation 2026 effective rate 7.2% (applied 11.8%); legal basis changed >50 times since Jan 2025 (B).

## Recoverability (A on law)
| Mechanism | Window |
|---|---|
| PSC | ≤300 days from entry and ≥15 days before scheduled liquidation |
| Protest (19 USC 1514) | 180 days after liquidation (liquidation ~314 days after entry) → ~18 months after entry |
| USMCA post-importation claim (19 USC 1520(d)) | 1 year from importation |
| Drawback | export/destruction within 5 years |
**Recoverable window ≈ 12–18 months, not 5 years.**
- Claims: "Section 232 metal-content over-valuation wedge is dead" since full-value basis (Apr 6, 2026) (B). [NOTE: disputed by analyst D — see reconciliation.]
- Contingency pricing already compressed: 4–8% on live refunds; 15–25% only post-180-day; flat ~$200 first entry + $125 each (C).
- Drawback niche and crowded (C).

## Hidden costs & risks
