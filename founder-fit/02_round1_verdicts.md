## Debate protocol — where the agents disagreed, and why

1. **C2: VC says "back it", market and killer say "shelve".**
   *Cause:* the VC scored founder fit and capital-light speed to revenue; the market analyst found the category already crowded and
   the incumbents (Jobber at $29–399/mo) bundling online quotes.
   *Resolution:* both are right on their own terms. The VC's success-case enterprise value ($50–80M) itself concedes that C2 is not a
   multi-billion business. The disagreement disappears once the objective (venture-scale) is applied. **Shelve.**
2. **C3: market analyst finds documented pain (lawsuits against Step Up in Florida; ClassWallet complaints; WV backlog); killer says Oklahoma has nothing to reconcile.**
   *Cause:* different geographies. The pain is real where the state pays schools through a vendor portal (FL, AZ, AR, WV) and
   absent where the credit is paid to parents (OK). And the documented pain clusters in special-needs schools.
   *Resolution:* the founder's home-market advantage applies exactly where the pain is weakest. **Shelve.**
3. **C1: customer agent sees a homeowner willing to pay $150–400 for a carrier-ready roof report; killer says nobody pays.**
   *Cause:* the killer evaluated the *passport subscription*; the customer agent found a *moment of acute need* (renewal, non-renewal,
   claim) where a homeowner will pay. *Resolution:* the payer and the moment are around **homeowners insurance**, not around the roof
   record. This is the insight carried forward.

## What round 1 taught (the carried-forward insight)

Every roof-related thread ended at the same place: **the money and the pain sit in the homeowner's insurance bill**, not in
roofing software. Oklahoma homeowners pay ~$5,378/yr, 4th highest in the US (A — LendingTree 2026). Convective-storm insured losses
have exceeded $50B for three straight years (A — Triple-I). Oklahoma law (36 O.S. §962) already requires admitted insurers to offer a
FORTIFIED wind/hail discount, up to ~42% depending on carrier filing (B — OID one-sheet and secondary summaries; statute text to be
read), and the state funds FORTIFIED roofs with up to $10,000 grants (A). And there is a multi-billion comparable for "a data-driven
specialist in the peril incumbents are retreating from": **Neptune Insurance, a flood MGA, IPO Oct 2025 at ~$2.8B; 2025 revenue
$159.6M, net income $37.4M, no balance-sheet risk** (A — SEC 10-K / Insurance Journal).

So round 2 investigates **C5: a hail-belt homeowners insurance agency built around the roof, with an MGA as the long-term
platform** — and, to avoid anchoring on my own reframe, a fresh independent second-sweep analyst who is not told about C5.
