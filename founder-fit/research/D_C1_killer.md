# Roof Record: Killer Memo (Agent C/H, 2026-10-01)

Labels: A = verified from a fetched/searched source, B = strong inference from sources, C = reasoned judgment, D = unverified assumption/speculation.

## Verdict: SHELVE the company as framed (roofer-pays passport, sell data to insurers). KILL the "sell data to insurers/buyers" revenue leg. One narrow reframing survives (bottom).

## Evidence gathered
- HB 2933 (roof-age 15+ yr non-renewal ban, aerial-image-only denial ban, independent inspection appeal) was introduced 2/2/2026, passed one chamber 3/30, marked dead 5/14/2026. [A] https://www.billtrack50.com/billdetail/1914433 ; text https://www.oklegislature.gov/cf_pdf/2025-26%20INT/hB/HB2933%20INT.PDF
  Implication: the one policy tailwind that would have made a homeowner-owned "proof of roof life" legally valuable is gone. Insurers remain free to use roof age + aerial imagery alone. [B]
- Insurers already buy roof age/condition from aerial imagery vendors: Verisk Roof Age and Roof Condition Score (1-5), CAPE Analytics (acquired by Moody's) roof age via historical imagery change detection, Nearmap, Cotality, Zesty. [A] https://www.verisk.com/products/roof-age/ ; https://capeanalytics.com/resources/roof-age-solution/ ; https://www.verisk.com/blog/roof-condition-transforming-property-risk-management-with-data-driven-insights/
  Imagery covers every roof at once, with no roofer participation, is consistent, and is not contractor-authored. That is the exact thing a ground record cannot be.
- Imagery errors are a real grievance (repaired roofs flagged damaged, shadows read as defects). [A, secondary blog] https://www.propertyinsurancecoveragelaw.com/blog/ai-roof-insurance-inspections/ This is the best argument FOR Roof Record, but see the "dispute remedy" reframing: it is a homeowner-pays, rare-event use case.
- Oklahoma already has a state-backed verified-roof pipeline: FORTIFIED Roof. Statute requires admitted insurers to discount certified FORTIFIED homes (reported up to 42% of wind/hail premium); an independent evaluator documents the build and IBHS issues the certificate; Strengthen Oklahoma Homes grants up to $10,000 per roof, now all 77 counties. [A] https://www.oid.ok.gov/release_011226/ ; https://www.oid.ok.gov/release_071026/ ; https://www.disasterresiliencenetwork.org/fortified
  Implication: for the highest-value record (FORTIFIED), the trusted party is the independent evaluator + IBHS registry, NOT the roofer and not a new app. Insurers already have a channel and do not need Roof Record to verify it. [B]
- Manufacturer warranty registration already creates the "record of homeowner, property, contractor, and covered products"; Owens Corning/GAF preferred contractors must register on the homeowner's behalf, and OC offers a homeowner lookup. [A] https://www.owenscorning.com/en-us/roofing/warranty ; https://warranty.owenscorning.com/pages/secure/help/HomeOwner/!SSL!/WebHelp/Claims/View_Warranty_Information.htm
- CompanyCam: 170k+ contractors, 1B+ photos, $2B valuation after Series C Aug 2025 (AI/construction push); $63-$249/mo; shareable galleries and PDF reports already exist. [A] https://fieldcamp.ai/reviews/companycam/ ; https://startupintros.com/orgs/companycam ; https://roofingsoftwareguide.com/reviews/companycam-review/
  A homeowner-facing "project record" is a feature a funded incumbent could ship in a quarter. [C]
- EagleView/Roofr/Hover already generate measurement reports (EagleView ~$33-87 each; Roofr from $0 plan, ~$13/report), so measurements are a commodity input, not a differentiator. [A] https://roofingsoftwareguide.com/comparisons/roofr-vs-eagleview/
- "Carfax for homes" graveyard: HouseFix closed 2012 (could not attract user base) [A] https://techcrunch.com/2012/10/08/housefix-the-carfax-for-homes-closes-up-shop/ ; Centriq shut its consumer product Jan 2026 after a late subscription pivot [A, secondary blogs] https://upkeepify.com/blog/centriq-alternative/ ; RealReports raised a $2M seed in 2024 selling a "Carfax for homes" [A] https://www.realestatenews.com/2024/02/07/carfax-for-homes-startup-secures-funding . Earlier I recall HomeBinder, Porch's origins, Homezada surviving as small niche players [D, not verified here].

## Attack by assumption

1. Demand from roofers is weak and it is a cost, not a revenue line. Roofers already hand over warranty certificate, invoice, and a CompanyCam gallery for free. Roof Record adds capture time (minutes per job in a busy-season crunch) for a benefit accruing to the homeowner and a third party years later. No roofer's close rate rises because a record exists. [C] The only roofer-side motive is marketing/lead-gen (referrals, re-roof reminders), which is a CRM feature, an already-crowded category (CompanyCam, JobNimbus, AccuLynx, Roofr). [B]
2. Insurers will not pay for, or defer to, contractor-supplied data. Roofers are the party insurers trust least: storm-chaser claim inflation, assignment-of-benefits abuse (Oklahoma has been an AOB/roofer-fraud battleground [D, not re-verified]). A self-attested record is an adversarial input. Insurers underwrite on data they control and can audit at scale (imagery, own inspection). "Verified" means verified by whom? If by the app, it is just the roofer's photos with a timestamp. [B/C]
3. Aerial imagery makes ground records mostly irrelevant for the buyer that has money. Insurers get roof age and condition for every policy for cents, retroactively, with no cold start. A ground record covers only roofs touched by participating roofers, going forward. [B]
4. Cold start across decades. A roof record's value is realized at sale, claim, or renewal 5-25 years out. Coverage in any underwriting-relevant sense (say >10% of Oklahoma roofs) needs thousands of roofers; Oklahoma has roughly 1.5M housing units and ~3-4% re-roof per year [D, rough], so even 100% of Oklahoma roofers yields perhaps 40-60k records per year, worth cents to an insurer who already has the data. [C]
5. Insurer data revenue is unproven and tiny at this scale. To reach even $250k ARR from data licensing you would need an insurer to value a record at a few dollars and cover 50k+ roofs. Realistic first insurer customers are small regional carriers who buy through a vendor, with 9-18 month procurement cycles, and no student founder can sit through that without revenue. [C]
6. Homebuyer-side sale is a non-market. Buyers do not pay for a roof passport; sellers will not pay for a record that can only expose defects; agents and inspectors already deliver the "roof info" (inspection report, seller disclosure). Oklahoma is not a roof-certificate state at closing. [B/C]
7. Seasonality and distribution. Hail season spring peaks; roofer attention is zero during storm surges, and the post-storm wave is exactly when low-quality storm-chaser outfits (the worst reputational cohort) flood in and accept free tools. Fragmented SMB roofers have low software budgets (CompanyCam at $63+/mo is considered a stretch by some), sell door-to-door, and churn. [B/C] Storm-chaser contagion: a "verified" badge that chasers also carry is worthless to good roofers, and a gated badge shrinks the TAM. [C]
8. Incumbent response. CompanyCam ($2B, AI roadmap), manufacturers (GAF, OC portals), EagleView/Verisk (own the insurer relationship) can each add a "homeowner roof record" with distribution Roof Record lacks. The FORTIFIED/IBHS pipeline also already issues a state-recognized artifact for the best-valued roofs. [B]
9. Founder fit. Strengths: iOS, design, trust brand voice (_founder_profile.md). Gaps: no roofing credibility, no insurer relationships, student bandwidth, no capital, B2B2B sales into trades and carriers is the opposite of an app-craft advantage. Greenturf gives some trades exposure but landscaping is not roofing. The product (a capture app) is the easy 10%; the hard 90% is distribution and a trusted buyer. [C]
10. Legal. If the product drifts into helping homeowners dispute claims or advising on coverage/damage valuation, it approaches unlicensed public adjusting (Oklahoma requires a public adjuster license; the founder has none). Roofers cannot legally negotiate claims either, and a platform that facilitates it may inherit exposure. Also PII/photo data privacy and "verified" claims creating liability if a record is wrong (negligent misrepresentation). [B; statute specifics D, verify with counsel]

## Single most dangerous assumption
"Someone with money, beyond the homeowner, will pay for a contractor-originated roof record before it reaches massive scale." (Insurers/buyers will value roofer-supplied data over, or in addition to, imagery they already get.) If false, there is no revenue, and the roofer has no reason to do extra work.

## Cheapest falsification test (2-3 weeks, ~$0, student-compatible)
- Call 8-10 underwriters/claims or product people at Oklahoma-active carriers (State Farm agents are not decision makers; target regionals like Farmers Alliance, Oklahoma Farm Bureau, Shelter, Mid-Century, plus imagery-vendor reps) with a single question: "If a roof arrived with a roofer-captured, time-stamped record (photos, permit, FORTIFIED cert, warranty), would it change underwriting, pricing, or a claim outcome versus your imagery? What would it have to include or be signed by to count?" Success bar: at least 3 of 8 say "yes, specific outcome" AND name a procurement path. Anything else is a fail.
- In parallel: 15 roofer interviews/cold calls: "Would you spend 5 minutes per job and pay $X/month to give homeowners a verified record?" Bar: 5+ say yes without a discount; also ask what they already send (CompanyCam link? warranty PDF?).
- Pre-mortem check: ask 5 homeowners who just re-roofed if they kept/can find their warranty and photos. If most say "the roofer emailed it," the problem is already solved.

## Reframing that survives (barely)
"FORTIFIED paperwork and grant-compliance copilot for Oklahoma roofers." Roofers who do FORTIFIED jobs (grant up to $10k, reported premium discounts, sizeable demand in 2026) must follow a documentation protocol with an independent evaluator (photos at set stages, fastener spacing, deck attachment, sealed deck, etc.). Roofers have an immediate, paid-adjacent pain (no certificate = no grant payout = no money), a state program creating real volume, a trusted third-party verifier already in the loop (so the insurer-distrust problem is bypassed), and the homeowner passport becomes a free by-product. Revenue from roofers/evaluators per job ($20-50) or small monthly fee; no insurer sales needed. It is also a narrow wedge a design-strong iOS builder can ship and test quickly, and it generates a verified dataset as a side effect. Caveats: FORTIFIED volume depends on grant funding continuing [B], IBHS/FORTIFIED has its own evaluator tooling that could expand [C], and market size is small (hundreds to low thousands of jobs per year in Oklahoma) [D]. Test: ask 5 FORTIFIED-certified roofers and 3 evaluators how they currently document and what breaks (30 minutes each).

## Final call
SHELVE as pitched. Do not build the insurer-data business. Spend 2-3 weeks on the two interview tests above; PURSUE only the FORTIFIED-documentation wedge if at least 3 of 5 FORTIFIED roofers/evaluators describe a painful, unsolved workflow and would pay. Otherwise KILL.
