# W2_C5: Strategic Killer / Contrarian teardown
Thesis: Phase 1 = FORTIFIED/verified-roof independent homeowners agency in Oklahoma (carrier commissions). Phase 2 = hail-belt MGA writing only verified resilient roofs on carrier/reinsurer capacity (Neptune-for-hail).
Date: 2026-10-01. Founder: Elliot Greenlaw (no insurance background, student, Edmond OK).

Evidence labels: A = primary source / regulator / filing read directly. B = credible secondary (trade press, vendor-neutral). C = vendor blog or my inference from numbers. D = unverified / from memory / assumption. Tool note: search results were thin on several items (stated where so). Nothing below labeled A that I did not see on a primary page.

---

## 0. The 60-second kill

1. The one thing that made Neptune work (flood has a monopoly-ish federal incumbent, NFIP, that is slow, mispriced and shrinking; private flood is a greenfield) does not exist in Oklahoma hail. Hail is already the #1 priced peril in the state; every admitted carrier prices roof age/material today. (B: Oklahoma premiums avg ~$5,858 projected 2026; roof is "single most important factor"; https://www.liveinsurancenews.com/oklahoma-tornadoes-hailstorms/8571928/ , https://roofvista.com/resources/guides/roof-insurance-canceled-age-2026)
2. The FORTIFIED discount is STATUTORY in Oklahoma. Admitted insurers must give it (36 O.S. 961/962), "up to 42% on the wind and hail portion." (B/A-ish: https://zoellnerinsurance.com/save-on-homeowners-insurance-in-oklahoma-with-fortified-home-program/ , statute cited by OID one-sheet https://www.oid.ok.gov/wp-content/uploads/2024/05/OKReady-onesheet-discountincentive.pdf; I could not parse the PDF). Your "FORTIFIED edge" is already handed to every competitor by law. An MGA cannot out-discount a mandatory discount, and an agency cannot differentiate on it.
3. The addressable FORTIFIED market is tiny. OID celebrated its 500th FORTIFIED home on Sept 1, 2026, after roughly 20 months of grants (A: https://www.oid.ok.gov/release_090126/ ; program funded by insurers, B). Oklahoma has ~1.5M owner-occupied homes (D, census-ish memory). 500 = ~0.03%. Grants are "up to $10,000", first-come, funded by the industry in rounds (A: https://www.insurancejournal.com/news/southeast/2026/07/13/877378.htm title; OID release_071026).
4. Phase 2 needs a fronting carrier + reinsurer + actuary + rate/form filings in a state that goes file-and-wait on July 1, 2027, with the founder having zero insurance credibility. Pure fantasy at his stage.

Verdict up front: **KILL as framed (venture-scale agency to MGA). SHELVE only the narrow reframe in section 9.**

## (a) Carrier appointments for a brand-new agency with no book

- Carriers directly appoint on premium-volume minimums and loss-ratio history; a zero-book agency gets declined by anyone good. That is why aggregators exist. (B: aggregator pitch itself says "new agents having problems getting markets to write new business", https://www.siaa.com/faqs/ , forum https://www.insurance-forums.com/community/threads/siaa-alliance-for-independent-insurance-agents.60519/)
- Smart Choice: no startup fees, 100+ carriers, "access within 24 hours", but you write under THEIR code; commission split ~70/30 agent/aggregator per a 2026 comparison (C: https://insuranceproagencies.com/insurance-aggregators-comparison-2026 ; https://www.smartchoiceagents.com/).
- SIAA: ~90/10 at SIAA level, then your master agency takes another cut; "effective rate depends on the local master agency" (C, same comparison; B: https://www.siaa.com/faqs/).
- So Phase 1 starts with a 10-30% haircut on a commission that is already 8-15% (see c). On a $5,858 policy at 12% new / 9% renewal and a 70/30 split: new-year ~$490, renewals ~$370/yr to you. (C: computed.) Aggregator carrier menus in hail states skew to E&S/non-standard and regionals, NOT State Farm/Allstate/Farmers/USAA (captive/direct). The good risks sit in channels you cannot access. (D: not verified which carriers each aggregator offers in OK; the search did not return carrier lists, you must check.)
- Who you are NOT competing with on price: the four incumbents control ~60% of Oklahoma (State Farm ~30 points of that) via captive agents (B: https://kfor.com/news/local/small-group-of-insurers-have-60-of-oklahoma-market-power/). You get the residual market: exactly the roofs they declined or non-renewed. That is adverse selection (f).

## (b) Carrier availability: is there anything to sell?

- Oklahoma ~2% non-renewal rate, 10th-worst state, vs 0.66% in 2018 (B: search summary, source in https://www.insurancebusinessmag.com/us/news/breaking-news/who-will-fix-oklahomas-unacceptable-home-insurance-market-571617.aspx).
- Carriers refuse roofs >15 years at any price; typical OK wind/hail deductibles avg $6,044 (B).
- A new OK law now bars non-renewal solely for roof age >=15 if an authorized inspector certifies >=5 years life (B: same Live Insurance News / OID Dec 2025 legislative package https://www.oid.ok.gov/release_121025/). This is a tailwind for roof inspectors, but note it is a LEGAL FORCE on carriers, not a paid-for service for you; inspectors already exist.
- Availability itself is not zero: Goosehead (1,100+ franchises, 150-200+ carriers) is selling OK homeowners and franchising OK (B: https://www.goosehead.com/homeowners-insurance/home-insurance-oklahoma/). So a market exists for the 15+-year-roof homeowner, but Goosehead is already the scaled, venture-backed, brand-built version, with real carrier relationships.
- Finding gap: I could not verify the current list of carriers accepting NEW homeowners business in OK through independent channels (D). That is the cheapest thing to test.

## (c) Commission economics and CAC

- Commissions: new 12-15% (some 12-20%), renewals 8-10% (some 10-15%) (B/C: https://myindependentagent.com/resources/insurance-agent-commission-structures , https://insifter.com/commission-benchmarks.html). I found NO evidence of broad carrier commission cuts in 2025 (honest correction of the premise). Ohio avg 13.2% in 2025 (B). So the commission-shrinkage attack is weak; do not lean on it.
- Commissions are % of premium, and Oklahoma premiums are high (~$5.9k). That is the one genuinely good number in this thesis: a $5.9k policy at 10-12% is $590-700/yr gross, strong vs. other states. (C)
- But: HB 3781 file-and-wait from 2027 plus Florida-style rate cuts (B: Florida approving cuts, https://www.insurancebusinessmag.com/us/news/property/florida-approves-more-home-insurance-rate-cuts-as-doubledigit-requests-pile-up-591320.aspx) means premium (and your % of it) is more likely to flatten than compound. (C)
- CAC: shared HO leads $10-20, exclusive $30-65, live transfer $50-95; cost per bind ~$120-250 at 15-25% quote-to-bind (C: vendor blogs, https://www.getinsureleads.com/blog/how-much-do-home-insurance-leads-cost , https://foundrycro.com/blog/insurance-advertising-benchmarks-2026/). Vendors selling leads publish the optimistic bind rates; independents routinely report far worse, 5-10% (D). At 8% bind on $50 exclusive leads = $625 CPA = roughly one full year of your split commission, before service cost, E&O, salaries, and the 3-5 year retention you need to be profitable. Payback 12-24 months, with the cat-year non-renewal risk making retention (the whole agency business) NEGATIVE in exactly the markets you chose. (C: computed)
- The correct reading: unit economics are workable for a scaled, license-holding agent who gets referrals and retains. They are not a venture business and cannot be bootstrapped by a student buying leads.
- FORTIFIED-specific revenue ceiling: ~300 grant homes/yr statewide (500 over ~20 months; C). Even 100% capture at ~$500 first-year commission = ~$150k/yr gross before the 30% aggregator cut. Nobody gets 100%. Realistic capture (3-5%) is pocket money. And those homeowners already HAVE a policy: you are asking them to change agent of record.

## (d) The graveyard

Digital agencies / marketplaces:

- Gabi sold to Experian for $320M (Nov 2021, at the top of the market; B: https://www.project-a.vc/blog/nsurtech-gabi-is-acquired-by-experian). It was auto, comparison-shopping, and ended inside a data company.
- Young Alfred acquired by Credible (Dec 2021; B: https://www.insurancejournal.com/news/east/2021/12/15/645722.htm). Covered (Denver, 2016) still operating small; I found no evidence of a shutdown or breakout (B: https://pitchbook.com/profiles/company/182557-18). Pattern: digital personal-lines agencies top out as acqui-hires/lead-gen assets. None became a standalone franchise. The one that did (Goosehead) did it via a franchise/sales machine, not tech. (C)
- Sources to read further: I did not find hard data on Matic's growth, so treat that as unverified (D).

Insurtech carriers/MGAs on cat:
- Hippo: hail/convective storms in Texas and Colorado produced a 178% gross loss ratio in its homebuilder-heavy book in 2023; response was higher wind/hail deductibles, non-renewals, rate hikes. Combined ratio 137.8 (2024) -> 113.1 (2025): still >100 after a "good" year (A/B: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1828105/000182810523000072/hippo-20230630.htm , https://www.insurancejournal.com/news/national/2026/02/25/859559.htm). Hippo is the exact proof that "tech + better data + new homes" did not tame hail.
- Florida: Weston was the 5th insolvency of 2022; $94M net losses in two years plus reinsurance cost spike (A: https://www.insurancejournal.com/news/southeast/2022/08/04/678662.htm). That's hurricane, but same failure mode: thin capital, reinsurance repricing.
- Vesttoo: >$4B of fake letters of credit; fronting carriers (e.g., Clear Blue) scrambled; Porch's HOA was put under TX supervision (B: https://www.businessinsurance.com/article/20230724/NEWS06/912358812/Fronting-insurer-Clear-Blue-severs-further-ties-with-insurtech-Vesttoo , https://www.marshberry.com/resource/vesttoo-fraud-investigation-another-issue-for-fronting-carriers/). Fronting survived and hybrid fronting did ~$28B GWP in 2025 (B: https://www.insurancethoughtleadership.com/commercial-lines/hybrid-fronting-model-reshapes-reinsurance). So capacity is available TODAY; the counter-evidence to my own attack: 2026 capacity is soft/abundant (B: https://blog.ryanspecialty.com/may-2026-us-property-insurance-review). That cuts both ways: easy to get capacity, but capacity is cheap because everyone is chasing the same few good risks, and it vanishes after a bad hail year (D, standard cycle).
- Not verified (D): Swyfft, Kin, Openly loss data. Search returned nothing on hail losses. Don't cite.

## (e) Convective storm is not flood

- Flood: Neptune is a distributor/underwriter with no balance-sheet risk, backed by AXA XL, Fortegra, Palomar; IPO at $20, raised $368M, 260k policies (B: https://www.reinsurancene.ws/neptune-insurance-announces-20-per-share-ipo/ , https://www.insurancejournal.com/news/southeast/2025/10/02/842201.htm). Its edge: NFIP is the incumbent and is slow; flood is low-frequency/high-severity with elevation-certificate-grade data (a few pre-computable attributes drive risk). Neptune's multiple relies on lack of cat in the book and a fee-for-underwriting model; Neptune has not been tested by a large flood year (C).
- Hail: high-frequency attritional, roof age is already the dominant rating variable, every carrier already filters on it; reinsurers raised SCS attachment points in 2023 and inflation is eroding that (B: search summary Aon: $127B SCS insured losses 2025, 27% above long-term average; https://insurify.com/homeowners-insurance/news/severe-convective-storms/). "Verified resilient roof" is a refinement of what carriers already do, not a new risk class.
- FORTIFIED evidence is WIND, not hail:
  - The only big study I found: University of Alabama CRIR, ~40,000 coastal Alabama properties, Hurricane Sally. FORTIFIED cut loss frequency 55-74%, severity 15-40%, loss ratio 51-72% (A/B: https://culverhouse.ua.edu/news/2025/05/crir-study-reveals-hurricane-sallys-effects-on-fortified-homes/ , https://www.insurancejournal.com/news/southeast/2025/05/28/825141.htm). That is HURRICANE wind, coastal Alabama. Zero hail.
  - IBHS's own FORTIFIED hail page cites NO claims data for hail loss reduction (A: I fetched https://fortifiedhome.org/article/hail-protection-that-works/ and it gave testing methodology and ratings only). I found no published hail claims study comparing FORTIFIED/IR vs standard roofs on loss ratios. That absence IS a finding: the load-bearing performance claim of Phase 2 has no public evidence (C).
  - IBHS lab tests: many "Class 4" shingles perform worse than the label implies against realistic ice hail; ratings range Excellent to Poor (A/B: https://ibhs.org/ibhs-news-releases/ibhs-shingle-performance-against-hail/). IBHS 2025: repeated sub-1-inch hail + weathering makes shingles ~10x more susceptible to later severe hail, and IR shingles performed "only marginally better" in cumulative-damage scenarios (B: https://www.insurancejournal.com/news/southeast/2025/10/02/842108.htm). That directly undermines "resilient roof = better loss ratio."
  - Cosmetic damage: some carriers pair IR credits with cosmetic-damage exclusions (B: IBHS/insurer summaries). If the carrier excludes cosmetic, the roof's real hail benefit is partly captured through a contract change, not underwriting skill.
  - The OK program's headline requires the "High Wind and Hail" designation (needs IBHS Good/Excellent shingles), so at least the spec targets hail (A: OID releases). But the spec being hail-targeted is not evidence of loss-ratio improvement.
- Conclusion: the central empirical claim is unproven for hail. Keep as D until a dataset exists.

## (f) Adverse selection

- Who raises their hand for FORTIFIED? Homeowners who (1) need a roof anyway, (2) just took a hail claim or got a non-renewal for roof age, (3) get a $10k grant: the cohort is roof-need-driven. It could be GOOD (new roof, mitigation-minded) or bad (just-had-a-claim, high-frequency location). I found no data either way (D).
- Your funnel (FORTIFIED/verified-roof marketing) selects for homeowners who already know hail is a problem for them = classic hail-loss-prone ZIPs. A new, small, aggregator-fed agency also gets the 'declined by captives' flow. Net: expect negative selection on location and claim history, partly offset by new-roof quality (C).
- Phase 2 MGA only writing verified roofs ALSO selects on one variable; carriers already see roof age via aerial imagery vendors (D: I believe CAPE/Zesty/EagleView-type data are widely used; not verified here), so the "verified" signal is largely ingested by the incumbents' own pricing.
- Moral hazard of the statutory discount: 42% off wind/hail premium is already conferred; if the roof is certified, the carrier is forced to give it, which lowers premium (your commission %) and gives you nothing.

## (g) Regulation

- Producer licensing: easy (pre-licensing + state exam + background check). Not the barrier. (B)
- MGA: Oklahoma defines MGA by authority to appoint producers and terminate appointments for an insurer, and requires an MGA license (A-ish: https://law.justia.com/codes/oklahoma/title-36/section-36-1435-2/). But an MGA is only as useful as the paper behind it: carrier (fronting) with an Oklahoma admitted license or E&S/surplus lines at 6% tax, 3 admitted declinations required (B: https://www.oid.ok.gov/regulated-entities/surplus-lines/). Admitted path = rate/form filings by the carrier, now with HB 3781.
- HB 3781: signed May 2026, effective July 1, 2027. Moves OK from use-and-file to file-and-wait: increases filed 30 days before effect, with supporting data; Commissioner can demand actuarial data; HO/auto/dwelling rate increases are posted publicly (A/B: https://www.okhouse.gov/posts/news-20260514_1 , https://beinsure.com/news/oklahoma-pc-insurance-rate-review-law-changes-insurance-filings/). Effect on your thesis: it slows the correction of an under-priced hail book, i.e. exactly when an MGA with a thin margin needs pricing agility after a bad season. Washington-style Cal-trap warning: https://www.insurancebusinessmag.com/us/news/breaking-news/is-oklahoma-about-to-walk-into-the-insurance-trap-california-has-been-trying-desperately-to-get-out-575815.aspx . Also the E&S route avoids filing but loses the statutory-discount distribution hook and the consumer protections political tailwind.
- OID is politically hostile to carrier rate increases AND hostile to non-renewals. An MGA that cuts a hailed ZIP after a storm will meet that hostility with a ~$0 balance sheet. (C)

## (h) Founder

- Zero insurance credibility; carriers, fronts, reinsurers, and master agencies buy on track record, loss-ratio history, and licensed principals. A student with iOS apps and a landscaping side project is not on that list (A: founder profile).
- Student time, no capital before revenue (A: profile). Phase 2 realistically requires: a chief underwriter and an appointed actuary-grade pricing person (~$300-500k combined comp, D), a fronting carrier agreement (usually demands $ capital/collateral and 5-15% fronting fee, D), reinsurance placement, and $10M+ of runway to survive 24-36 months before fee income (D; the user brief itself says $10M+). None of this is something the founder can learn into in a semester.
- Strengths do exist but are mismatched: design/UX and iOS are not the bottleneck in insurance distribution. Trust-over-pressure brand and genuine Christian ethos are real differentiators for retail, but retail is the part with 30% aggregator cut and $120-625 CAC, the weakest link.
- Time-to-first-dollar: license ~1-2 months, aggregator appointment ~days to weeks, first commission 30-60 days after bind; but the first dollar is trivial and proves nothing about the thesis (C).

## 7. Single most dangerous assumption

**"Verified resilient-roof status carries information or leverage the market is not already pricing, so a specialist can win either distribution (Phase 1) or loss ratio (Phase 2)."**

Why this one: it is common to both phases; the OK statute already hands every consumer the FORTIFIED discount through every admitted carrier; carriers already price roof age/material; and public FORTIFIED loss evidence is wind-based (hurricane), with hail data absent and IBHS's own lab work showing IR shingles only marginally better against cumulative hail. If this fails, Phase 1 is a commodity agency with a 30% aggregator haircut and a ~300-home/yr niche, and Phase 2 has no underwriting edge to sell reinsurers.

## 8. Cheapest 30-day test that falsifies it (total cost < $300 and ~25 hours)

Week 1: Pull 10 OK FORTIFIED-roof contractors (listed on OID/IBHS lists) and 10 independent OK agencies. Ask each two questions by phone: (1) "In the last 12 months, how many FORTIFIED-certified homes did you place or re-place, and did the carrier discount differ from the statutory max?" (2) "Which carriers accept NEW homeowners business in OK today on a 12-year-old shingle roof, and how many declined?" Record answers.
Week 2: Apply to Smart Choice and SIAA (no fee at Smart Choice per https://www.smartchoiceagents.com/) just to see the OK homeowners carrier menu, hail deductible/ACV roof terms, and commission schedule in writing. (Do not need the license to get the menu quote; you do to bind.) Ask for the actual HO new/renewal commission schedule, not marketing ranges.
Week 3: Email OID (okready@oid.ok.gov, A: https://www.oid.ok.gov/release_090126/) and ask for: carriers participating in the statutory discount, number of grants per round, and any loss data they hold on certified homes. Ask IBHS/UA CRIR for any hail-peril FORTIFIED claims study.
Week 4: Score. 
- FALSIFIED (kill) if ANY of: (i) the discount is identical across carriers (statutory), so no agency differentiation; (ii) no aggregator-accessible carrier writes HO on 12+ year roofs in OK, or commission <10% new/<8% renewal after split; (iii) no hail-peril claims data for FORTIFIED exists anywhere; (iv) fewer than 3 of 10 contractors say they'd hand you leads over their existing agent partner.
- SURVIVES only if: a carrier or agency shows hail-specific claims data favoring verified roofs AND at least 4 of 10 contractors would route grant homeowners to you AND an aggregator-accessible carrier writes the book at >=12% new commission.
Prior (C): I expect (i) and (iii) to hold. That means kill.

## 9. Reframes that survive (ranked, honest)

1. **Roof-evidence tooling sold to people who already have licenses and books** (independent OK agencies, FORTIFIED contractors, or carriers' underwriting teams): iOS/LiDAR roof-capture + FORTIFIED paperwork/photo-evidence + grant-application assistant, SaaS or per-job fee. Uses the founder's actual edge (iOS, LiDAR interest, design), avoids licensing and capital, reveals demand before any insurance risk. FORTIFIED requires certified evaluator photo/documentation workflows (B/A: OID program materials) so a documentation tool has a real, narrow job. Weakness: TAM is ~300 homes/yr in OK today (C), so the product must generalize to AL/LA/MS/SC/NC/TX FORTIFIED states (D: those state programs exist, e.g., Louisiana Act 533 report appears in search https://ldi.la.gov/docs/default-source/documents/propertycasualty/act-533-fortified-discount-report-2025.pdf ).
2. **Join an existing Oklahoma independent agency as a licensed producer/analyst** for 12-24 months (nights/summers), learning appointments, retention, and loss data first-hand. Costs nothing, buys the credibility whose absence is the real constraint.
3. A hail-peril FORTIFIED claims study with a carrier partner (data play). Valuable if it exists; reinsurers would pay for it; but needs a carrier friend you don't have.
Not surviving: standalone digital agency; MGA before year 5.

## 10. Verdict

**KILL** the thesis as stated (agency-to-MGA). Specifically kill Phase 2 as a plan; kill Phase 1 as a venture. **SHELVE** reframe #1/#2 only if the 30-day calls show contractors want a documentation tool. Do not spend capital, do not buy leads, do not chase an MGA license.

Confidence: ~80% (kill). Biggest uncertainty: I could not read the OID discount PDF or find carrier-level OK commission schedules; if OK admitted carriers' discounts differ materially from the statutory max or an aggregator carrier pays 15%+ with 5-year retention, Phase 1 moves from "commodity" to "marginal side business", still not a venture.
