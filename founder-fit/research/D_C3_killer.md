# D_C3 Killer: School-choice tuition billing + reconciliation software for small Christian schools / microschools (and SGO back office)

Date: 2026-10-01. Agent C/H (Contrarian + Strategic Killer).
Evidence labels: A = primary/official source fetched or quoted; B = credible secondary/news/advocacy summary; C = vendor marketing or inference from partial evidence; D = my reasoning/no direct source (treat as hypothesis).

## 0. Bottom line
VERDICT: SHELVE as framed (KILL the "reconcile school-choice money" wedge; at most keep a tiny tuition-billing/design play alive if an unrelated cheap test passes). The reconciliation pain mostly does not exist where Elliot lives (Oklahoma), the ESA-state pain is owned by state-mandated platforms, and the SGO back office already has at least four named startups months before launch.

## 1. Attacks, ranked by lethality

### 1.1 The Oklahoma money flow leaves almost nothing to reconcile (A) -- most lethal for the founder's home market
- Oklahoma PCTC is a state income-tax credit paid to the parent, not a school-facing grant. OTC mails warrants (checks) to the taxpayer and to the school; the parent endorses the check to the school, or keeps it if tuition is already paid. Two installments, one per semester, each half of expected tuition or half the max credit. Credits are $5,000-$7,500 per child by income. Source: https://www.okpsaedu.org/blog/faqs-oklahoma-parent-tax-choice-credit (A/B: a private-school association summarizing OTC rules).
- The only school obligation is a participation agreement and a Nov 1 report of attendance, credit amounts received and student IDs to OTC (same source). That is a spreadsheet export, not a product. A school bookkeeper or FACTS already does it. (D: no evidence anyone pays for this.)
- Scale of Oklahoma: 39,587 students, $247.8M credits this year, cap raised $250M to $275M effective Aug 13, 2026 (https://www.kgou.org/education/2026-05-06/oklahoma-parental-choice-tax-credit-cap-expanded-from-250-million-to-275-million, B). So demand is real, but the workflow is "check arrives, parent endorses."
- Implication: the founder's home-state wedge is the weakest possible reconciliation market. The "reconciliation" story only has teeth in ESA states (AZ, FL, TX, WV, AR, etc.), which are not his backyard and are covered in 1.2.

### 1.2 In ESA states the state's platform owns the money flow (B/C)
- ESA programs run through state-selected payment platforms: ClassWallet (used in Florida, Arizona, West Virginia, Arkansas and others), Odyssey (Texas TEFA; built ESA-first), Student First Technologies, Step Up's own MyScholarShop. Sources: https://schoolchoiceusa.org/payment-platforms (C), https://getesapaid.com/compare-esa-payment-platforms (C, vendor-ish), https://www.accessible-educationtx.com/post/official-tefa-provider-applications-now-open-2025-odyssey-updates-for-vendors-and-private-schools (B).
- Schools/microschools must become vendors inside those portals and get paid by the portal. The administrator is incentivized to own the vendor-side experience (invoicing, payout status). A third-party reconciler can only sit downstream of someone else's CSV/API that it has no contract to access (D). Platform-side API access is the single point of failure: it is politically and contractually controlled by the state/administrator.
- Note the vendor-portal guidance already targets microschools directly ("Texas ESA vendor portal ... guide for microschools", https://www.biggieschools.com/blog/the-texas-esa-vendor-portal-opens-dec-9-a-step-by-step-guide-for-microschools, C), so there is already content/consulting filling this pain, though not a dominant software product (D, I did not find one).

### 1.3 Incumbent bundling: FACTS/Nelnet owns the buyer (B)
- FACTS: largest K-12 tuition payment-plan provider; 650,000+ families, $1.8B tuition/year, 3,500+ private/parochial K-12 schools; sub-organization of Nelnet; particularly prevalent in faith-based schools; bundles SIS and financial aid (https://www.capterra.com/p/148464/FACTS/ and https://makerstack.co/reviews/facts-review/, B). Nelnet also sells tuition management (https://nelnet.com/capabilities/payment-technology/tuition-management/, A: vendor page).
- Any real tuition-billing pain, including "tag which payments came from a scholarship," is an obvious feature add for FACTS, which already has financial-aid workflows, a school-choice program team and distribution into Christian schools (D inference). I could not find a published FACTS-ECCA product, so this is a prediction (D), but it is a cheap feature for them and a fatal one for a solo founder.
- Below FACTS: Brightwheel, Blackbaud, Stripe/Square invoicing, QuickBooks, Venmo and church-office Excel are the real competitor at the tiny end. For a microschool with 12-40 students, "software" competes with "a Stripe payment link plus a Google Sheet," at $0 (D).

### 1.4 ECCA/SGO side is already crowded before launch and will be dominated by big SGOs (B/C)
- SGO software competitors exist already: SGO Software (scholarships.software/sgosoftware.com), SGO HQ, ClearPath, SGO Pro, SGO Guide (https://sgosoftware.com/, https://sgoguide.com/sgo-software, https://sgopro.app/; C: vendor pages). Product scope described: donor receipts, income verification, award prioritization, separate-account accounting, disbursement, reporting. That is exactly the "back office for new SGOs" idea. At least four exist before Jan 1, 2027 (A that they exist as sites; C on their quality/traction).
- Large SGOs already exist and will move first: Step Up For Students already spun up a dedicated federal-credit SGO ("Step Up, Step Further Scholarship Fund") (https://www.edchoice.org/school-choice/programs/federal-tax-credits-for-scholarships-ftcs/ and Dykema piece, B). ACE, diocesan funds, Scholarship America and existing state tax-credit SGOs have donor lists, audits and systems (D inference; Step Up runs its own platform per above).
- SGO economics are brutal: about 10% admin cap covering "staff, technology, audits, legal, fundraising, donor stewardship, income verification, and reporting" (https://eftccredit.com/learn/sgo-90-10-rule-compliance, B). Software spend is a slice of a slice; new small SGOs are low-volume. Also required: audits, no earmarking, 10+ students, more than one school, separate accounts per state (same source, B; https://www.dykema.com/news-insights/the-new-federal-scholarship-tax-credit.html B).
- Per-return cap is $1,700; JCT 10-year cost $25.9B (Ballotpedia, B). Money exists, but donor acquisition (not software) is the SGO bottleneck, and donors prefer established brands (D).
- Treasury proposed regulations were promised "by end of September 2026" (https://eftccredit.com/learn/sgo-90-10-rule-compliance family of pages, B/C; EdWeek Sept 2026 lists many unresolved issues: https://www.edweek.org/policy-politics/federal-tax-credits-for-k-12-scholarships-what-we-know-and-dont/2026/09, B). Building to rules that are not final is a risk and big SGOs have lawyers to absorb it.

### 1.5 Political/legal fragility (mixed: partly weaker than hypothesized)
- ECCA opt-out risk is smaller than feared: IRS says more than half the states signed up; ~30 states as of Aug 2026, including Colorado and Virginia (Dem trifectas), plus Kentucky and North Carolina via veto override; NY's Hochul announced intent (https://www.irs.gov/newsroom/more-than-half-the-us-states-signed-up-to-participate-in-the-federal-scholarship-tax-credit-program-enacted-under-the-one-big-beautiful-bill A; https://ballotpedia.org/State_participation_in_the_federal_K-12_education_tax_credit_program B; https://eftccredit.com/states B). So "Democratic governors opt out" is a weak kill argument. Honest label: my hypothesis was partially falsified.
- Repeal risk is real but conditional: S. 4297 (Kelly) with 29 Democrats + 2 independents, April 2026; repeal "unlikely without a change in chamber control" because enacted via reconciliation (https://news.ballotpedia.org/2026/04/24/senate-democrats-introduce-bill-to-repeal-federal-education-tax-credit-scholarship/, B). A 2028 unified Democratic government would be the risk window; a product with 2027-28 revenue could be killed in year 2-3 (D). Opt-in is also a state decision that can flip with governors/legislatures each cycle (D).
- ESA court risk is live: Utah Fits All ruled unconstitutional by a district judge, program continuing pending Utah Supreme Court, no ruling as of Oct 2026 (https://www.abc4.com/news/politics/inside-utah-politics/utah-fits-all-scholarship-ruling-on-hold-program-can-continue-until-utah-supreme-court-decides/ B); South Carolina Supreme Court struck its voucher program 3-2 (https://www.k12dive.com/news/south-carolina-ruling-vouchers-esa-program/726954/ B); Montana blocked its disability ESA Dec 2025 (https://dailymontanan.com/2025/12/09/court-strikes-down-education-savings-accounts-for-students-with-disabilities-as-unconstitutional/ B); but Wyoming's Supreme Court upheld its ESA in 2026 (https://www.edchoice.org/2026-wyoming-supreme-court-upholds-esa-program-defended-by-edchoice-legal-advocates-and-institute-for-justice/ B/C advocate source). Net: court outcomes are a coin flip by state constitution; each loss deletes a state's TAM overnight.
- Oklahoma PCTC: I found no lawsuit (searched; none surfaced; label B, absence of evidence). Oklahoma risk is instead fiscal/political: cap expansion fights, reports that $19.3M went to wealthier households, governor pushing to uncap (Yahoo/Oklahoman summaries, B). Tax-credit structures are legally sturdier than ESAs (D), so OK is the safest state and also the one with the least reconciliation pain (see 1.1). That is the cruel trade-off: legally robust = low pain; high pain = legally fragile.

### 1.6 Customer: tiny budgets, church boards, calendar-keyed sales (B/D)
- Microschool profile (1,000-school sector analysis): 60% rely mainly on family tuition, 66% give sliding-scale discounts, ~50% have families receiving state choice money, ~100 hours/year on compliance (https://microschoolingcenter.org/news-blog/sa2026, B). Pay-ability is low: a 20-student school at $6k is $120k/yr revenue; a $100-$300/month tool is 1-3% of revenue and competes with free (D).
- Christian schools are often governed by church boards/elders with annual budget cycles; adoption decisions cluster March-June for August start, and a missed window means 12 months (D). With a student founder part-time, 1-2 selling windows per year means maybe 2-3 learning cycles in the 18-month runway (D).
- Churn: only 20% of microschools are 6+ years old, 45% are 3-5 years, 33% under 3 (same source, B). Reasons for closure include lease expirations, founders' children aging out, and leaders lacking business skills (https://www.k12dive.com/news/what-you-need-to-know--about-microschools/827601/, B). That implies high logo churn (D), cutting LTV even if product-market fit exists. Counter: leaders who close often reopen (same source), so a founder-relationship channel could persist.

### 1.7 Payments, money-transmission and fraud (D, with partial A basis)
- Handling funds on behalf of schools or SGOs implies either a licensed processor/aggregator model (Stripe Connect/Treasury, Modern Treasury) or money-transmitter exposure; SGOs must also keep separate segregated §25F accounts per state (https://eftccredit.com/learn/sgo-90-10-rule-compliance, B). A solo unlicensed student founder cannot hold funds; he would have to be a software layer on someone else's rails, which commodifies the product (D).
- ACH return/chargeback risk on tuition (one failed $800 payment kills a microschool's month) is borne by the school but blamed on the vendor (D).
- Fraud: scholarship money plus income-verification (300% AMGI) and EIN-impersonation creates attack surface; SGO disqualified-person screening is mandatory (B). One incident is reputationally fatal in a trust-based Christian community (D).

### 1.8 Data privacy (D)
- Children's data, family income documents (for SGO income verification), tax IDs and bank details. Private schools are mostly not directly subject to FERPA unless they take federal funds, but parents expect FERPA-like handling; COPPA applies to under-13 data collection, state student-privacy laws (Oklahoma's Student DATA Act applies to public education agencies; applicability to vendors of private schools is unclear, D). Income-verification stores are the worst liability: a breach of 300% AMGI documents for thousands of families is existential for a seed-stage company with no security staff (D). SOC 2 is a cost an SGO customer will ask for.

### 1.9 Founder credibility (D)
- Student, no education-industry or finance/compliance background, no licences; his own profile says he cannot raise large capital before first revenue (/home/user/New-Ideas/founder-fit/research/_founder_profile.md, A). Selling to church-board treasurers and SGO executive directors who are accountable to auditors and donors is a trust sale to people who do not buy from a 21-year-old they have not met. Offsetting asset (D): Christian network trust is a real channel and design quality is a visible differentiator in a sector of ugly software. But it does not solve the payments/compliance moat gap.

## 2. Single most dangerous assumption
That small schools experience school-choice money as an operational reconciliation problem they will pay to solve (rather than a parent-endorses-a-check or portal-pays-you event, with the real pain being parent-facing billing and family cash-flow). Everything (the OK wedge, the ESA wedge, the SGO wedge) rests on "there is a manual, painful, recurring reconcile job at the school." My evidence says: in Oklahoma it is a check endorsement plus one Nov 1 report (A/B); in ESA states the platform pays the school (B/C).

## 3. Cheapest falsifying test (under 2 weeks, $0)
Phone 15 Oklahoma private-school / microschool bookkeepers or administrators (OPSAE member schools, Oklahoma Christian-network schools), with no pitch. Ask: "Walk me through the last PCTC check and last semester's tuition ledger. Where did it take longest, who did it, how many hours, what tool?" Kill criterion: fewer than 5 of 15 describe 3+ hours/month of scholarship-related reconciliation pain, or fewer than 3 would pay $100+/month in a stated-price ask. Secondary test: ask 3 ESA-state vendors (Texas/Arizona microschools via the vendor-portal communities) whether Odyssey/ClassWallet payout reports already tie to their ledger. Pass requires pain in ESA states, which then means the founder must sell out of state, which is a different company.

## 4. Reframings that might survive (ordered by plausibility)
1. Drop reconciliation; sell beautiful, trust-first parent-facing tuition billing for Oklahoma Christian micro-schools (payment plans, sibling discounts, "scholarship expected" line item with a clean statement), with scholarship tagging as a feature, not the thesis. Honest note: this re-enters FACTS/Stripe competition and has a small, churn-prone ACV (C/D). Viable only as a lifestyle/side business, not venture.
2. A free/cheap "PCTC participation toolkit" (Nov 1 report generator plus parent guidance) as top-of-funnel for #1. It is a lead magnet, not a company (D).
3. Services-plus-software for one or two new Christian SGOs in OK/TX (concierge back office, white-labeled on someone else's SGO platform). Competes with four named SGO software vendors (C) but trust and relationships could beat features. Low capital, but 10% admin cap means tiny budgets, and he is exposed to repeal/regs risk (D).
4. Do not pursue: building a payments or fund-holding product; income-verification document storage.

## 5. Verdict
SHELVE. Re-open only if the 15-call test shows real hours-per-month pain in Oklahoma or if a Christian SGO founder offers to be a design partner and pay (cheap, binary evidence). As scoped ("reconcile school-choice money"), probability of becoming a durable company is low: crowded SGO tooling (C), state-owned ESA rails (B/C), incumbent bundling (B/D), no Oklahoma reconciliation pain (A/B), and policy cliff risk in 2028-29 (B/D). The ECCA opt-out attack failed (30 states in), and I record that honestly; the stronger kills are structural, not political.
