## 1. Candidates

### C1. OTD Check for Credit Unions: "Honest car buying" as a member benefit (B2B2C)
- **Problem / who pays:** Members overpay at dealers; CUs want to protect members' auto loans (a core CU product) and differentiate. Payer = credit union (per member / per funded loan / flat SaaS), not the consumer.
- **Evidence:** CUs already buy car-buying services (TrueCar, CU Direct) (A, links above); F&I profit $2,547/unit proves the overcharge pool (A); CR says extras can exceed $4,000 (A). CU willingness to pay for transparency-flavoured tool vs TrueCar's lead model: C.
- **Existing / gaps:** TrueCar/CU Direct are dealer-network lead products: they steer to network dealers and do not audit add-ons/OTD or pre-approve the buyer's out-the-door number against the loan. CarEdge is consumer-direct. Gap: a loan-aware OTD audit (bring me the dealer's buyer's order, we flag add-ons, doc-fee outliers, payment packing, check it against your approved loan) delivered inside the CU's app/site. (C)
- **Why now:** CARS rule gone so consumers have no federal OTD backstop (A); FTC warning letters make "fees" front-page (A); CUs competing with captive/indirect lenders for loans (B).
- **30-day wedge:** Productise the existing OTD app as "OTD Check": scan/enter a buyer's order -> itemised flag list (add-ons, doc-fee vs state norms, rate markup vs pre-approval). Launch free in Oklahoma. Email 10 small OK credit unions (member-benefit pilot, free 60 days, success measured by loans funded). Do not promise legal accuracy; label "educational".
- **Path to $100M:** Needs ~5,000 CUs/banks at ~$20K avg (= $100M) or per-loan fees ($50-100 per funded loan x ~1M+ loans). There are ~4,500 US CUs (D, not verified here). Plausible but long: more likely a $5-20M business; $100M needs lender channel (auto lenders, insurers, employers) too. Honest odds low.
- **Moat:** Proprietary buyer's-order dataset + CU relationships; modest. Regulation-dependent (state-by-state) rules engine.
- **Founder fit: 8/10.** Direct reuse of OTD; iOS/design strength matters in member-facing UX; trust brand matches; Oklahoma CU network is local and often faith-friendly; no licence needed if framed as information (but check state "unauthorized practice"/broker laws: C). Weakness: enterprise sales cycle for a student; CU compliance/vendor-risk reviews are slow.
- **Verdict: STRONG (best fit of this lens), but slow to monetise.**

### C2. Dealer-paid "All-In Price" publishing + verification (honest-dealer certification)
- **Problem / who pays:** Honest dealers lose to bait-price advertising; FTC wants advertised price to include mandatory charges (A). Payer = dealers ($/mo for widget that publishes verified OTD, with consumer-side proof).
- **Evidence:** FTC 97 warning letters (A); CarEdge index shows consumer demand for dealer ratings (A); one dealer-industry blog says transparent dealers report selling more (B/D: vendor claim, no data). https://www.onedealerlane.com/blog/300-or-bust-the-f-i-rule-that-protects-your-dealership-now-that-washington-isnt-coming-to-save-you
- **Existing / gaps:** Dealer website vendors and digital retail suites already show "price" widgets; CarEdge rates dealers but sells to buyers; no trusted third-party "verified OTD" mark that consumers recognise. (C)
- **Why now:** Enforcement risk makes compliance a selling point; no federal rule means dealers who want to advertise compliance have no standard. (C)
- **30-day wedge:** Pick 5 Edmond/OKC independent dealers who sell on reputation; free "verified all-in price" page using OTD calculator; ask buyers to confirm. Success = 3 dealers paying $99/mo.
- **Path to $100M:** ~16,000+ franchised + 20,000+ independents (D); at $300/mo x 25,000 dealers = $90M; unrealistic penetration. Lead-gen/badge pay-to-play is the trap: if badges are paid, trust collapses (C). Honest path maybe $10-30M.
- **Moat:** Brand trust only if verification is independent; paying customers conflict with it.
- **Founder fit: 6/10.** Brand and design fit, trust message fit; but dealer sales is hostile territory for a student, and conflict of interest undermines the "trust over pressure" brand.
- **Verdict: MAYBE (structural conflict; use only as a CU-channel add-on, not primary).**

### C3. Oklahoma Christian-school / microschool operations stack (tuition + PCTC/ESA paperwork + enrolment)
- **Problem / who pays:** New and small private schools and microschools need billing, scholarship/credit reconciliation, parent portal, compliance reporting. Payer = school (SaaS $/student/mo or % of tuition processed).
- **Evidence:** OK program ~40K students, cap up to $275M (A); accredited schools 119 -> 192 (B); universal ESAs in AZ/FL/TX with microschool eligibility (A); FACTS dominates 6,500 of 17,000 schools but draws complaints about fees (A); Microschool Ledger exists, so it is not empty (A).
- **Existing / gaps:** FACTS/Blackbaud/Veracross are heavy and built for established schools; Prenda is a vertical network, not neutral software. Gap: lightweight, beautiful, mobile-first ops for 10-100-student faith-based schools, co-ops, and church-run schools in Oklahoma, handling PCTC tuition timing and family receipts. (C)
- **Why now:** Texas EFAs launch Fall 2026 (A), Oklahoma growth, new school formation wave (B).
- **30-day wedge:** Free parent-facing "tuition and credit tracker" for 3-5 Oklahoma church schools or co-ops (Oklahoma Christian University networks); charge $2/student/mo after one semester. Pure software; no licence needed.
- **Path to $100M:** Needs national scale: e.g., 20,000 small schools x $5K/yr = $100M, but small schools have tiny budgets and FACTS embeds payments (payments revenue is the big pool: 2.9% of $1.8B is ~$50M+ at FACTS scale: C). Payments-take model is the real route; requires payments partner (Stripe Connect) and compliance. Honest odds: low but nonzero; realistic $5-20M.
- **Moat:** Switching costs once billing lives in the system; community trust in niche; weak initially.
- **Founder fit: 8/10.** Faith credibility is a real edge in selling to Christian schools (relationships through OCU, churches); design edge vs legacy ugly software; Oklahoma timing perfect; mobile-first parent app uses SwiftUI. Risks: handling money (payments compliance), school sales cycles tied to school year (next buy window ~Jan-Jun 2027), regulation moving (program caps, deadlines).
- **Verdict: STRONG.**

## 2. Cross-cutting lessons (honest)
1. **Who pays decides everything.** Consumers pay small amounts for these problems (CarEdge $49/$999 is the best evidence of WTP, A). Payers with real budgets are institutions: credit unions, schools, dealers. (C)
2. **Lead-gen is the trap** in cars, repair, home services, funeral, medical: the revenue source (sellers) conflicts with the trust promise. The founder's brand cannot survive it. (C)
3. **Faith advantage is real only where trust and community relationships are the sales channel** (Christian schools, CUs, churches as distribution), not in crowded consumer apps or commodity church software. (C)
4. **Regulatory vacuum, not tailwind:** CARS Rule is dead (A), so "compliance-driven demand" is weaker than a year ago; enforcement is case-by-case. (C)

### Top 3 and strongest failure reason

**1. C3, Oklahoma Christian-school/microschool ops.**
Strongest failure reason: small schools have tiny budgets and FACTS/Microschool Ledger own the money flow; to reach $100M you must win payments take-rate nationally, which means handling funds, compliance, and years of sales cycles tied to the school calendar, while program rules (caps, deadlines) can shift politically.

**2. C1, OTD Check for credit unions.**
Strongest failure reason: credit unions buy slowly and already have TrueCar-style programs that cost them nothing and generate rate/loan flow; a student with no references may never clear vendor-risk review, and without a dealer-network, "audit only" may be nice-to-have rather than must-buy.

**3. C2, Dealer-verified all-in price (or fallback C6).**
Strongest failure reason: dealers earn ~$2,500 per car from F&I, so the dealers who most need transparency are least willing to pay for it; the few who do are a small niche, and any paid badge compromises the independent trust that makes the product valuable.

Recommended path (C): run C1 and C3 in parallel as 30-day experiments in Oklahoma: C1 free OTD Check + 10 CU emails; C3 free tuition/credit tracker for 3 church schools. Whichever gets a paying (or at least committed) institution first becomes the venture; kill the other.
