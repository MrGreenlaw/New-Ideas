# W3 Killer: Roof-Record App to Insurance Agency (Agent H, 2026-10-01)

Evidence labels: A = primary/authoritative source fetched or quoted (statute, SEC filing, regulator); B = credible secondary (trade press, vendor docs); C = marketing/blog or inference from B; D = my own reasoning/assumption, unverified. Only `_founder_profile.md` was read. Search was limited (WebSearch snippets only; no deep fetches), so several carrier-behavior claims are D and must be tested, not trusted.

## Verdict: SHELVE the thesis as written (KILL the "free app -> roofer-sourced re-shop" wedge; keep one narrow variant alive, see end)

## Top 10 failure reasons, ranked

1. **The re-shop reason is mostly eliminated by carrier data (C/D).** Verisk sells Roof Age combining permits, aerial imagery, assessor records and claims; Verisk and CAPE sell condition/age scoring that carriers use for underwriting and renewal ([Verisk Roof Age](https://www.verisk.com/products/roof-age/), B; [Carrier Management/Verisk roof claims](https://www.carriermanagement.com/news/2026/05/29/288511.htm), B). A city permit plus new aerial imagery lets the incumbent re-rate at renewal without a roofer app. The "verified record" is not scarce data. The homeowner's trigger to shop (new roof = lower price) arrives anyway from the incumbent, at the next renewal, with zero effort. Whether it arrives automatically in OK is unverified (D), but the direction is clear.
2. **The discount is small and applies to a slice of premium (C).** Roofer blogs say Class 4 discounts are about 5-20% and apply only to the wind/hail portion, not the whole premium ([Parker Brothers Roofing](https://www.parkerbrothersroofing.com/blog/slash-your-insurance-bill-how-much-can-impact-resistant-shingles-save-you-in-oklahoma), [State Farm discounts](https://www.statefarm.com/insurance/homeowners/discounts), C/B). Against a ~$5.3-5.9k premium ([Lemonade/aggregators](https://www.lemonade.com/homeowners/explained/homeowners-insurance-cost/), C), realistic saving is a few hundred dollars, and most of it is available by simply calling the existing carrier. A different carrier has to beat the incumbent after the incumbent already re-rated.
3. **Roofers have no incentive to hand over customers, and the one incentive that works is illegal or radioactive (A).** Oklahoma law bars inducements and rebates in connection with insurance ([36 O.S. 1204](https://law.justia.com/codes/oklahoma/title-36/section-36-1204/), [OID Bulletin 2025-01](https://www.oid.ok.gov/bulletin-no-2025-01/), A). Roofers are separately barred from paying deductibles or compensating insureds ([59 O.S. 1151.30](https://law.justia.com/codes/oklahoma/title-59/section-59-1151-30/), A). Referral fees to an unlicensed person are permitted only if they are not based on the purchase of insurance and the person does not discuss policy terms ([IIAT summary](https://www.iiat.org/agency-operations/insurance-laws-regulations/insurance-laws-regulations-sales-marketing/rebating-and-referral-fees), B). So the natural "split the commission with the roofer" model is out, and a flat per-lead fee is a compliance minefield that needs OID counsel. Without money, the roofer gets only a free app, and his main app need (estimating, photos, CRM) is already served by CompanyCam, AccuLynx, JobNimbus (D).
4. **The roofer's brand risk is asymmetric (D).** If the roofer's customer is upsold insurance and the new policy denies a later claim, the roofer is blamed. Roofers already fight the "storm chaser" image and Oklahoma restricts their claim-adjacent conduct (A, above). Many already have an agent friend or a family agent who sends them contractor-insurance business (D; [okc roofing contractor insurance agencies](https://www.lifelonginsuranceagency.com/oklahoma-roofing-contractor-insurance/), C).
5. **Gratitude and inertia with the claim-paying carrier (D).** The homeowner just got a $15k check from State Farm or similar. Switching immediately after a claim is a known behavior (customers get surcharged or non-renewed after a claim, so some are shopping), but a larger group stays put. Also, a recent claim is on the CLUE record for years, so the "most insurable moment" claim is half true: the roof is new but the claim history is not (D, needs test).
6. **Carrier appointments and quality of book (D, unverified).** A brand-new agency with no book and a lead source of "roofer-referred post-claim customers" is a thin pitch to standard carriers writing OK wind/hail. My searches found no public data on appointment minimums or roofer-sourced business restrictions, so this is an open risk, not a fact. In practice, new agents often go through an aggregator/cluster to get access, which cuts commission and gives carrier-level control to a third party. Adverse selection fear (post-hail-claim homes shopped around by the contractor who just got paid by the claim) is plausible grounds for carriers to throttle or refuse (D).
7. **Unit economics are thin and lumpy (D, arithmetic).** Premium ~$5.5k x 10-12% net of aggregator split = ~$450-650/yr/policy. A roofer doing 150-300 roofs a year, with 10-15% of customers converting, yields 15-45 policies/roofer/year = $7k-$29k/yr of commission per roofer after year one. You need ~1,500-2,000 in-force policies for ~$1M revenue, i.e., ~50-100 active roofer partners at that conversion, in a state where homeowners retention is already pressured. Storm-surge servicing (claims handling calls, endorsements, non-renewals) arrives when revenue is lowest per hour.
8. **Seasonality and storm-year volatility (D).** Roofing volume tracks hail events, so lead flow is clustered and unpredictable. Carriers raise rates and cut appetite after bad years. Verisk notes roof claims topped $30B in 2024 and roof costs are rising ([Verisk](https://www.verisk.com/company/newsroom/u.s.-roof-claims-costs-reached-over-$30-billion-in-2024-underscoring-evolving-risks/), B). Carrier appetite for hail-belt homeowners can vanish after a bad year, stranding the book.
9. **Founder fit (D, from profile).** Engineering student, no license, no insurance background, part-time, no capital; the agency half needs a licensed co-founder who carries E&O, compliance, and carrier relationships. The founder contributes the part (app design) that is commodity and the co-founder contributes the part that is the real business. The risk is that the "company" is really the agent's agency with a free app attached, with the founder as a junior partner on equity-light terms. Student schedule vs. a business whose peak is storm season is a poor match.
10. **Copy risk and Porch precedent (A/B).** Porch's path (inspector software to insurance distribution to reciprocal) got it there but at high cost: in 2023 its HOA carrier took a $48.2M charge from the Vesttoo reinsurance fraud and Porch had to inject $57M for a $49M surplus note ([Porch 10-K FY2023](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1784535/000178453524000019/prch-20231231.htm), A), then moved HOA into a reciprocal exchange in January 2025 ([Business Wire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20250107440222/en/Porch-Group-Completes-Formation-of-Porch-Insurance-Reciprocal-Exchange-and-Corresponding-Sale-of-Homeowners-of-America-Insurance-Company), A). The recovery (2025 revenue and EBITDA growth, per [8-K Feb 2026](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1784535/000178453526000004/prch-20260211xexx991.htm), A) shows the model can work, but only with a big carrier-side balance sheet and years of data. Porch already sells to inspectors and movers. CompanyCam/AccuLynx/JobNimbus can add a "roof record" PDF in a quarter, and Verisk/CAPE already own the data (D). The MGA/reciprocal endgame needs millions in surplus and regulatory approval; it is not a student-founder-reachable stage.

## Single most dangerous assumption

**That a homeowner with a newly installed roof, referred at job completion, will switch carriers (or buy a new policy) with the roofer-affiliated agency at a meaningful rate (>=10%).** If the incumbent re-rates automatically and the saving is small, conversion collapses and everything downstream (roofer value, commission, scale) is zero. Roofer willingness is the second assumption but is moot if homeowners do not convert.

## Cheapest 30-day test (no app, no license needed to run the first half)

Goal: measure homeowner demand and the real price delta before building anything.

Week 1-2 (no licence needed, interviews only): recruit 30 Oklahoma homeowners who had a roof replaced in the last 12 months (via 3-5 friendly roofers, Facebook/Nextdoor, OKC/Edmond neighborhoods; do not discuss policy terms or offer anything of value). Ask: did your premium change at renewal after the new roof? Did the carrier ask for permit/photos? Did you shop? What would make you switch? Collect the renewal declarations pages (before and after) where they will share them.

Week 2-4 (with the licensed co-founder or a cooperating agent running the quotes; the founder only analyzes): run 15 real quote comparisons on those homeowners across 4+ carriers.

Pass/fail thresholds (all must hold to proceed):
- At least 40% of the 30 report that their premium did NOT already fall or their carrier did NOT already credit the new roof (i.e. incumbent did not auto-reprice). Fail if >=60% say the carrier already adjusted.
- Median quoted saving on the 15 comparisons >= $500/yr (or >= 10% of premium) vs. the incumbent's post-roof renewal price. Fail if <$300.
- At least 25% of the 30 say they would switch for that saving, and at least 8 of 15 quoted would accept a bind.
- At least 3 of 5 roofers approached say they would introduce the agency without any compensation (flat-fee or commission-share excluded) and 0 say it would hurt their brand. Fail if <2 agree.
- Co-founder confirms in writing that at least 2 carriers writing OK homeowners would take roofer-introduced business at a normal commission (call 4 carriers/aggregators). Fail if the only available market is surplus lines or an aggregator that takes >35% of commission.

## Version I would fund (only if the test passes), as an alternative to the stated thesis

Not "free app for roofers". Instead: a licensed agent-led "new roof insurance review" service sold through 3-5 roofers as a plain, compliant introduction (counsel-approved flat marketing fee not tied to the sale, or no fee) with the homeowner-facing deliverable being the roof record (a PDF/web page, built in a weekend with existing tools). The record's job is to give the homeowner the evidence to get credits from the incumbent or a better carrier. Focus on a measurable outcome (dollars saved), page the carrier for credit, track conversion. Bootstrap budget under $5k: the agent's licence/E&O, a landing page, a CRM. Build the iOS app only after 3 roofers use the PDF weekly and conversion >=10%. Skip the MGA/reciprocal until the book exceeds ~$2M premium and a reinsurance partner exists. If the test fails the 40%/$500 thresholds, KILL: the roof record is a feature that carriers and incumbents already provide, not a business.
