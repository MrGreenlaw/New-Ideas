## 1. Market size realism
- Homeowners premium, 8 states: $36.6B (A, per brief).
- Blended commission to the agent ~10-12% (new 12-15%, renewal 8-12%, brief). Total agent-commission pool ~ $3.7-4.4B/yr (C).
- Independent-agent share of homeowners is roughly a third or less; the rest is captive (State Farm, Allstate) and direct (D). Pool actually contestable by an independent: ~$1.2-1.5B (C/D).
- Auto adds a larger premium pool (my guess ~$90-110B in these 8 states, D), but the margin is thinner and it is more commoditized and direct-dominated. Contestable independent commission ~$2-3B (D).
- Headline TAM: "$5-7B of commissions" is real but meaningless. Nobody captures it.
- SAM (the honest filter): households that (a) get a renewal shock, (b) will shop, (c) hold a home a carrier you can access will write, (d) will trust a student-founded agency. Maybe 8-12% of households shop in any year (D). SAM ~ $150-400M of annual commission (C/D). Hail-exposed older roofs shrink this further because carriers won't write them.
- SOM: $1M revenue needs ~1,700-2,300 households at $430-600 net per year (arithmetic on the brief's $420-650 gross and 10-30% aggregator split, C). That is a tiny slice of SAM. The market is not the constraint. Distribution cost and carrier access are.
- Verdict: market is big enough for a $5-30M business; not evidence for a $1B one. The TAM slide is not a reason to invest.

## 2. Founder-market fit
Honest read: weak on market, strong on craft.
- Strengths: ships iOS himself; design and copy craft; trust-first brand voice is genuinely rare in insurance; licensed already; cheap to iterate; some local-services exposure via Greenturf; real moral conviction (matters over a 10-year grind).
- Gaps: no insurance background (a licence is not a book of business); student, part-time; no distribution relationships with realtors or lenders; no capital; no sales track record, and this business is sales and carrier relationships first, software second. His products to date (OTD, Locus, Friendly Fire) are consumer apps with no evidence of paid acquisition at scale (D, from profile).
- "Honest, says stay put" is a brand, not a moat, and it lowers conversion revenue by design.
- What co-founder changes my mind (all must hold):
  1. Experienced P&C agent with a portable, verifiable personal book or 2-3 producing relationships (a documented $300k+ commission book, with carrier-contract ownership clear and no non-compete problem).
  2. Has already placed hail-exposed Oklahoma/Texas homes and holds direct appointments (not only aggregator access), so appetite risk is theirs to solve.
  3. Full-time, equity vesting over 4 years, with a lender/realtor/roofer network that has actually referred business before.
  4. Willing to run the agency P&L and carrier relationships so Elliot builds product and brand.
  Without all four, I see a student with a license and an app. With them, I see a normal small agency with a good front end, which is a business, but a small one.

## 3. Capital needed
- Before first revenue: ~$15-40k (C): E&O (~$2-5k), entity/legal, aggregator onboarding, licensing/appointments, domain/app/AI extraction costs, local content. Elliot can fund this. The first commission lags bind by 30-60 days, so budget 3 months of float.
- To first ~100 households (proof): $40-120k incl. modest ads and the co-founder's partial pay (C).
- Before $1M revenue: $0.8-1.5M (C). Logic: ~2,000 households; blended CAC $300-600 (brief: $120-250 vendor to $600+ realistic) = $0.6-1.2M if paid; organic/referral lowers it; plus two to three salaries (~$250-350k over 18-24 months); the cash payback on CAC is 9-18 months, so growth consumes cash. A $500k-1M pre-seed is the right size for roughly 18 months of runway to ~$300-600k ARR, not to $1M.
- It is plausible to reach $1M revenue with ~$250-400k if mostly referral/co-founder book-driven. That is a different business (a traditional agency).

## 5. Defensibility
- Day 1: none. Brand, UI, "honest" copy are copyable in a weekend by Policygenius, a Goosehead or any local agency.
- Year 1-2: a small local reputation and a reviews flywheel in Edmond/OKC. Real, not a moat. Referral relationships with 5-15 realtors/loan officers/roofers: sticky but personal, and they walk with the human.
- Year 3-5, only if volume exists: placement data (which carrier takes which home at what price) could lower cost-per-bind. This needs thousands of placements across carriers to matter; at under ~5k households the data is anecdote. That is the one moat with a path; earliest credible date ~2029-2030 and it only exists if Redbud binds >1,000 homes/yr (D).
- Regulation: Oklahoma anti-rebating (36 O.S. 1204) and no unlicensed commissions (1435.14) constrain competitors too, they are not a moat.
- MGA: not defensible until loss data exists; the brief itself notes no hail-specific FORTIFIED evidence. Needs a fronting carrier, capacity, and actuarial credibility. Years away, and a different company.

## 6. Enterprise-value scenarios (comps)
Anchors: independent agencies transact ~8-11x EBITDA at $1-3M, 10-13.5x at $3-10M, 12-16x at $10M+ (B/C, CT Acquisitions/Insurance Journal). 73% of deals are PE/hybrid buyers, 60-80% cash at close, rest earnout on retention (B). Goosehead public: ~$1.6-2.4B market cap (brief) on $365M revenue and $113.6M adj. EBITDA = ~4.4-6.6x revenue and ~14-21x adj. EBITDA (C). Neptune ~$2.8-3.1B at IPO (B), a high-margin flood MGA, a much better business model than an agency. Policygenius: undisclosed (B); Gabi ~$320M (B-/D); digital agencies mostly sold or wound down at modest value relative to capital raised (D).

| Outcome | Revenue | EBITDA | Multiple | EV | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Lifestyle | $1.5-3M | $0.3-0.7M | 3-8x | $1.5-5M | local agency sale or keep; earnout-heavy; founder income $150-300k |

$1B+: need ~$70-80M EBITDA at 13-15x, i.e. ~$250M revenue at 30% margins, or a Goosehead-type public premium. That is roughly 500k+ households, multi-state agents or a franchise network, and a carrier-grade placement advantage that makes growth cheaper than paid leads. Or: a profitable MGA/carrier layer atop the agency (Neptune/Kin/Openly route) with proprietary loss data. Odds from this starting position: ~0.1%.
$10B: not conceivable as a P&C personal-lines agency (no agency has been valued there; the only $10B+ insurance-intermediary names are global commercial brokers, per my memory, D). It would have to become a carrier or a platform with underwriting. I would not underwrite it, and I'd be suspicious of anyone who pitches it.

## 8. 90-day milestones that earn a $500k-1M pre-seed
1. Co-founder signed, full-time or committed date, equity vesting, book portability documented.
2. Live Renewal Check (iOS + web) with 150+ real Oklahoma declarations pages uploaded and 40+ completed re-shops.
3. 20-30 bound policies at blended CAC under $250 (target) with at least 40% from non-paid channels; commission statements to prove revenue (not LOIs).
4. 2+ direct appointments beyond the aggregator, with written appetite for 15+-year roofs or a documented reason why not.
5. 5+ referral partners (realtor/loan officer/roofer) with at least 2 that sent 3+ deals each, structured within 36 O.S. 1204 and 1435.14 (get an Oklahoma insurance attorney sign-off).
6. A retention proxy: of the "stay" recommendations, share of customers who return or convert later; and NPS/reviews.
7. A unit economics sheet from actual statements: net commission per policy, CAC, servicing time per policy, 12-month payback path.
No traction on 3 and 4 = no check from me.

## 9. Most likely reason I pass
Not the market. I pass because the unit economics of one-person-honest-agency (thin $/policy, $600+ realistic CAC, 12-18 month cash payback, carrier appetite risk on hail roofs) give no venture-scale path, and the team has no insurance distribution edge. Close second: Elliot's honesty positioning reduces conversion revenue and invites the question "why isn't this a feature at Goosehead, Policygenius or a local agency?" Third: a student cannot do this part-time against carriers and licensed competitors who do it full-time.

## 10. Recommendation for this founder: honest-pricing web/quote studio for local trades vs Redbud venture attempt
Advice: build the cash business now. Do not raise or burn for Redbud yet.
Why:
- It fits what he is already strong at (premium web design, copy, front-end, trust voice, Greenturf exposure) and needs no co-founder, no license, no carrier.
- Cash early: a $1.5-3k build + $100-300/mo care plan, 15-25 trades clients in year 1 is $40-90k with no capital, around his coursework (C estimate, D on conversion). It buys runway, references, and relationships with roofers, HVAC, and contractors, who are the same people who would later refer Renewal Check traffic.
- It preserves the option: insurance Renewal Check can run as a 5-10 hr/week experiment (free web tool, content, his license) while the studio pays bills. If 90-day milestones in section 8 appear and a real co-founder-agent appears, he can raise from stronger ground.
- Risks of the studio: low ceiling ($300-800k/yr is hard to exceed solo), churn of small clients, AI site builders compress prices. Still, the downside is a student with a paying portfolio, versus the Redbud downside of 18 months chasing unprofitable policies.
- Blunt: the studio is the better business for Elliot today. Redbud is a good idea in search of the right partner and capital sizing. Do the studio; hold Redbud as a validation sprint; revisit the day a proven P&C agent says "I'll quit my job for this."
