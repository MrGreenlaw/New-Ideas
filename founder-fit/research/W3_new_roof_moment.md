# W3. "The new-roof moment" hypothesis (hail-belt homeowners insurance at re-roof)

Date: 2026-10-01. Evidence grades: A = primary (statute text, regulator, filing, company filing); B = reputable secondary or named study; C = trade press, vendor blog, single secondary source; D = my assumption or estimate. Nothing here is legal advice. Items I could not verify are marked UNVERIFIED.

## Verdict: PARTLY SUPPORTED

Of the four sub-claims, (a) risk drops is supported, (b) peak carrier appetite is partly supported, (c) dissatisfied post-claim homeowner is weakly supported and Oklahoma law blunts it, and (d) nobody prompts a re-shop is plausible but untested. The legal route is workable only with a licensed agency partner and a carefully structured roofer relationship. The market is real but modest per policy. A bottom-up base case yields about $0.2-0.3M first-year commission in OK and about $2.6M in 8 states at steady state. This is a distribution business whose hard problem is roofer adoption and conversion, and no desk research can resolve that.

## 1. Re-roof volume

- US residential: about 5M roofs replaced per year, or about 7% of 75M single-family homes (C: RAKE ML and NRCA-attributed figure as relayed by search; I could not open the NRCA primary). https://www.rakeml.com/articles/american-homeowner-roof-replacement-statistics-a-deep-dive-into-the-20-billion-industry
- US asphalt shingle demand was about 154M squares in 2024 (B, Freedonia). Q3 2025 shipments were 40.4M squares, down 10% year over year (B, ARMA via Roofing Contractor). Freedonia says residential reroofing is about 81% of the residential roofing market, and it expects a post-2020-2023 surge hangover. https://www.freedoniagroup.com/industry-study/asphalt-shingles-us ; https://www.roofingcontractor.com/articles/101472-us-asphalt-shingle-shipments-fall-10-in-q3-2025
- Permit-based count: roughly 400-500k roofing permits per year nationally, peaking at 584,551 in 2023 (B, Shovels). This is an undercount because permit coverage is patchy: only 21.5% of Houston addresses have roofing permits on record, versus 74-78% in Denver and Colorado Springs. https://www.shovels.ai/blog/roofing-permit-data-insurance-roof-age/
- Oklahoma: no state count found. Tulsa County reported 6,774 roofing permits "last year" (C, vendor blog; period and scope unclear). OKC only began requiring roofing permits on 2025-08-01 (A, city). That means OKC permit data will become a usable lead and roof-age source from late 2025, but history is thin. https://www.okc.gov/News-articles/Roofing-permits-required-in-OKC-starting-Aug.-1
- Insurance-funded share: HIRI says insurance funds about 21% of roofing projects (C). III reports over 22% of residential replacements stem from storm damage (C). I found no OK- or TX-specific split. My read is that hail-belt states are well above the national 21%, but that is D.
- Roof life in hail-prone states is about 15 years versus 22 in milder areas (C). Verisk says 16 states had 20% or more of roofs hit by severe hail in 2025, including KS 51.8%, NE 47.1%, AR 41.9%, and OK and SD 44.4% (B). https://www.claimsjournal.com/news/national/2026/06/01/337878.htm

Owner-occupied units, ACS 2023 (B, via search summary; verify before external use): TX 7,048,369; OK 1,034,153; KS 792,366; NE 539,532; CO 1,594,191; MN 1,687,695; MO 1,758,041; AR 816,038. The 8-state total is 15,270,385.

## 2. Rating impact

- Class 4 / FORTIFIED discounts apply to the wind/hail portion of the premium only.
  - The OID one-sheet lists FORTIFIED Roof discounts from 2018 filings: State Farm 30%, Union Mutual 25%, Goodville and IDS 20%, Armed Forces and EMC 15%, Shelter 14%, Foremost 10%, Nationwide 6%, QBE 3%, and National Security 42% (A, regulator, but dated 2018). https://www.oid.ok.gov/wp-content/uploads/2024/05/OKReady-onesheet-discountincentive.pdf
  - OID says average savings are about $750 per year after FORTIFIED certification, with 500 homes completed as of 2026-09-01 and grants of up to $10,000 (A). https://www.oid.ok.gov/release_090126/
  - OID also says "many companies" give about a 25% discount for Class 4 shingles (A, consumer guidance). https://www.oid.ok.gov/getready11_2023/
  - Roofer-blog claims of 5-28% are C.
- Oklahoma statute: the user's "§6665" guess is wrong. The relevant law is 36 O.S. §961 (2017, eff. 2018), which I read in the statute text (A).
  - It requires a discount only when the insurer finds it actuarially justified.
  - It applies only to property certified to Appendix Y of the 2015 Oklahoma Uniform Building Code or FORTIFIED High Wind and Hail, and only to the wind/hail premium. Insurers that already offer a hail-resistance discount are deemed compliant.
  - The discount applies at the renewal following submission of certification.
  - So a plain Class 4 re-roof in OK has no statutory mandate. It is a voluntary carrier filing. https://oksenate.gov/sites/default/files/2019-12/os36.pdf
- Oklahoma 2026 package: OID announced mandatory FORTIFIED-roof discounts, a ban on non-renewing, refusing or reducing coverage solely because a roof is 15 or more years old (with an independent inspection appeal showing at least 5 years of life), and a ban on action based solely on aerial images (B: OID announcement page via summary). Whether each item was enacted and when is UNVERIFIED. If enacted, it weakens leg (b), because old-roof decline power shrinks. https://www.oid.ok.gov/release_121025/
- Texas: TDI Commissioner's Bulletin B-0030-98 and Order 98-0390 (1998) require credits for new UL 2218 roofs, delivered on a TDI-promulgated certificate of installation that the installer must give the policyholder (A). A secondary source cites Ins. Code §2253.002 (UNVERIFIED). Note that in Texas the roofer already hands the homeowner an insurance-relevant document. https://www.tdi.texas.gov/bulletins/1998/b-0030-8.html
- Roof-age penalties:
  - A ZestyAI analysis of more than 2,000 filings (about 60 carriers, July 2026) says 85% of Oklahoma carriers use ACV roof schedules, the highest in the nation. Nationally, top-10 carriers' ACV roof schedules went from 10% to 60% of policies between 2015 and 2025 (C, vendor analysis, not independently checked). https://www.liveinsurancenews.com/why-oklahoma-roof-claim-checks/8574132/
  - Example from the same source: a 15-year-old roof, $20,000 replacement, 50% depreciation, and a 2% wind/hail deductible on a $350k home gives a claim check of about $3,000 and $17,000 out of pocket.
  - A claim that State Farm goes ACV at 15 years in OK and that Allstate has a 15-year limited roof endorsement is C and unconfirmed. An Allstate "ACV over 10 years in Oklahoma, Oct 2025" item turned up with date inconsistencies, so I exclude it.
  - Counter-trend: Zesty says roof age in filings is plateauing and roof condition is growing about 32% per year (C). Carriers are moving to condition via aerial imagery. A new roof still scores well on condition, but the "roof year" field matters less than I assumed. https://zesty.ai/resource/evolution-of-roof-risk-assessment
- Loss lift: Verisk says roofs in moderate or poor condition have roughly 60% higher loss costs than good or excellent roofs (B). No usable Verisk or Cotality figure by roof age bucket turned up. The average roof replacement cost was $17,631 in 2025, up 33% (B, Verisk). Roofs are about 30% of claim estimate line items.

## 3. Do homeowners tell insurers? Roof-year staleness

- No quantified study of carrier roof-year staleness after a re-roof was found. This is a gap.
- Adjacent evidence:
  - BuildFax studied 391,309 homes with homeowner-supplied roof age. More than two-thirds understated roof age by more than five years, and over 20% by more than 15 years (B, 2013). https://www.claimsjournal.com/news/national/2013/08/22/235434.htm
  - A trade claim says inaccurate roof age costs insurers $1.31B in premium a year (C; weak sourcing).
  - Agent and insurer blogs say carriers will not know about a new roof unless told (C).
- Implication: the data problem runs both ways. Homeowners often under-report age, which is the opposite of staleness. The "carrier does not know" story is plausible but unproven. For insurance-funded replacements, the carrier already has the claim file, so the roof is known.
- Permit data now gives carriers same-month visibility where permits exist (Shovels, B), which will erode the information edge over time.

## 4. Post-claim switching and Oklahoma rules

- 36 O.S. §3639.1 (A, statute text): no cancellation, non-renewal or premium increase "solely because the insured filed a first claim" on a policy in force more than 45 days. Exceptions: nonpayment, fraud, willful or reckless acts, a change in risk that substantially increases the hazard, ordinance violations, and others.
- OAC 365:15-7-26 (A): "Homeowners policies ... shall not reflect a surcharge for weather related claims."
- OID Bulletin 2026-02 (A, via summary): 60 days' notice of non-renewal for homeowners, effective 2026-07-25. https://www.oid.ok.gov/bulletin-no-2026-02/
- My read: the law blunts the "dissatisfied after a hail claim and surcharged" story. A weather claim cannot be surcharged and a first claim cannot trigger non-renewal. Premiums still rise through base rates, ACV schedules and percentage deductibles. Oklahoma has the highest average premium (about $4,651 for a $300k home, Bankrate via Oklahoma Watch, B) and a 4% or higher non-renewal rate in southeast OK (B). https://www.kgou.org/show/oklahoma-watch-long-story-short/2025-05-21/long-story-short-hail-storms-do-not-explain-oklahomas-homeowners-insurance-rates
- Switching evidence:
  - J.D. Power 2024: 6.8% of home customers were actively shopping and only 2.2% switched (B). https://www.insurancejournal.com/news/national/2024/09/18/793368.htm
  - J.D. Power 2025: 47% had a rate increase; communication quality drives satisfaction (B).
  - J.D. Power claims study: dissatisfied digital claim experiences correlate with a 52% likelihood to leave versus 4% for excellent experiences. That is auto and home combined, not hail-specific (B). Average claims cycle is 44 days to final payment (B).
  - A "30% switch within five years of a claim" figure attributed to NAIC (C or D, I could not source it; do not use).
  - No Oklahoma hail-claim-specific switching data found.
- Market structure: OID says 113 active homeowners insurers, about 60 writing new business (B). There is competition to place into, but appetite for hail-belt homes is selective.

## 5. Competitors: roofer to insurance referral

- Porch Group: owns iRoofing (roofer software, acquired Jan 2021, B/C) and Homeowners of America (HOA, a TX-founded reciprocal, 22 states, Michigan the newest). Its insurance engine is fed by home inspection software and real-estate and mover data. The Q2 2026 call reports reciprocal written premium of $139.8M (up 16%), $255M for H1, a $600M 2026 target, 58,700 policies written in the quarter, and $2,383 premium per policy written (B, transcript). The call does not mention roofers or roof-trigger sourcing (B). Insurance Services revenue is 66% of RWP, which comes from management fees and policy fees, not agency commissions. This is a carrier-plus-MGA model, not the agency model in the hypothesis. https://www.fool.com/earnings/call-transcripts/2026/08/07/porch-group-prch-q2-2026-earnings-call-transcript/
- Roofing CRMs (AccuLynx, JobNimbus, Leap, Roofr): their insurance features are claim workflow, supplements and adjuster tracking, not insurance sales (C). https://roofingsoftwareguide.com/roundups/best-insurance-restoration-roofing-software/
- Searches for Openly, Kin, Hippo, Matic and Lemonade roofer partnerships returned nothing. "Hippo Roofing" is an unrelated Florida contractor. Absence in search is not proof of absence.
- "Insurance agencies for roofers" are commercial lines (general liability, workers' comp), a different product (C).
- Manufacturers (GAF, Owens Corning, CertainTeed) promote Class 4 shingles and certified contractors for the insurance discount (C), but I found no insurance-distribution program.
- Whitespace reading: either a real gap or a sign that roofers rank low as an insurance referral channel. I have no evidence to tell which (D).

## 6. Legality

Oklahoma:
- 36 O.S. §1204(8) (A) bars offering "any valuable consideration or inducement whatever not specified in the contract" to the insured or prospective insured, except in accordance with an approved rate filing. Paying commissions to licensed agents is carved out.
- 36 O.S. §1435.14 (A):
  - You may not pay a commission, service fee or "other valuable consideration" for selling, soliciting or negotiating insurance to anyone who needs a license and lacks one (A, B).
  - Subsection D permits paying "persons who do not sell, solicit or negotiate insurance," but not where the payment would violate §1204.
  - I found no express Oklahoma "fixed fee, not contingent on sale" rule in the statute text. The NAIC model approach, which I could not read directly, reportedly allows a referral fee only if it is a fixed amount that does not depend on whether the customer buys, and only if the referrer does not discuss policy terms (B: search summary of NAIC-related sources). Treat that as the safe design standard.
- Oklahoma's deductible-waiver law (HB 1940, effective Nov 1, felony; year given inconsistently across sources) bars roofers from paying or waiving deductibles in claim-funded jobs (C). It shows regulators watch roofer and insurance entanglement.
- Gift limit: a $100 gift allowance appeared in a search summary only (C, UNVERIFIED). Do not rely on it.

Texas:
- Ins. Code §4001.051(d) and §4005.053(c) authorize referral fees to unlicensed individuals when policy terms are not discussed and the fee does not depend on a sale (B, Insurance Journal and Texas statute summaries). https://www.insurancejournal.com/magazines/mag-features/2024/02/19/761025.htm
- The rebate and inducement rule is in Ch. 541 (§541.056 or similar). The summaries I saw were thin (C).

Giving free software to roofers:
- The inducement runs to the roofer, not the insured, so §1204(8) is not obviously triggered.
- The risk is that free software is value given to an unlicensed party in exchange for steering customers. If the benefit effectively depends on policies sold, it looks like contingent compensation to an unlicensed solicitor.
- Safer designs: roofer pays a market price or the software is truly independent of referrals; flat per-referral fee not tied to a sale; roofer never discusses terms or price; the homeowner app gives value (roof record, warranty, Class 4 certificate) regardless of quoting; and no gift to the homeowner for quoting.
- Counsel review in OK and TX is required before launch. The founder holds no licence, so a licensed agency principal or partner agency is needed.

Ceiling check: if every 8-state re-roof moved a policy at $3,500, that would be about $2.7B of premium per year. Base case captures about 0.8% of that ($21.9M premium). Compared with Porch's $600M reciprocal premium target, the base case is about 3.6% of it.

Read-through: unit economics are about $420-700 first-year commission per policy, so $100k a year of income needs roughly 150-250 new policies. In OK alone the base case does not support more than a side business, and the 8-state version is a small company. Revenue depends on roofer adoption (S) far more than on insurance mechanics. The ranges above are wide on purpose.

## 8. Biggest evidence gap

Conversion at the moment of roof completion. Nobody publishes how many homeowners would re-shop, or bind through an agency, when handed a quote by or through their roofer, nor how many roofers would adopt such software and keep promoting it. I also found no measured price delta from re-shopping with a new roof (the real value to the homeowner). A secondary gap is carrier roof-year staleness after a re-roof. Closing the main gap needs a pilot with 5-10 OK roofers and a licensed agency partner, measuring quote requests, quotes beating the current premium, and binds. Secondary gaps: Oklahoma-specific re-roof counts and the insurance-funded share, whether the 2026 OK roof-age and discount laws were enacted, and primary-source Texas statute cites.
