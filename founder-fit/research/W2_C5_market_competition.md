## 0. Bottom line (hard verdict)
- **Phase 1 (agency):** viable as a small, founder-sized business. It is not a venture-scale business and has no moat by itself (sec 2, 7a).
- **Phase 2 (MGA):** the thesis as written ("underwrite only FORTIFIED/verified roofs") has a near-empty addressable book today. About 400–500 FORTIFIED roofs exist in Oklahoma (sec 4). A broader "verified young roof" definition is needed, and that is the real product (sec 7b).
- **Neptune analogy is weaker than it looks.** Flood has a federal backstop, a mandatory-purchase driver and a clean peril. Convective storm is a volatile, attritional peril. Texas is also moving against roof-age-based selection (sec 1d).
- **Preliminary call: SHELVE as a venture. PURSUE only as a lean agency experiment** if Elliot can get 2–3 independent carrier appointments that actually write Oklahoma hail risk. If not, KILL.

### 1d. Regulatory facts (Texas verification, Oklahoma)
- **Texas VERIFIED, with a correction.**
  - Governor Abbott's directive letter was dated **Aug 24, 2026** (recommendations due Sep 14). It tells TDI to: have insurers incorporate FORTIFIED roof status in rates; bar refusal or non-renewal solely on property or roof age; ban price optimization; create a fraud task force; study claims costs. **A** (gov.texas.gov, fetched).
  - TDI Commissioner's Bulletin **B-0008-26, dated Sep 16, 2026**, *encourages* "meaningful, actuarially supported" loss-mitigation discounts (FORTIFIED named as an example) and requires SERFF filings for new discounts. It does **not** mandate discounts. **A** (https://www.tdi.texas.gov/bulletins/2026/commissioners-bulletin-b-0008-26.html, fetched).
  - So the premise "FORTIFIED in rating" is directive-level and bulletin-encouraged. It is not yet a mandated discount. A legislative change is planned for the next session.
  - **Implication for the thesis (D, inference):** if Texas bars refusing risks on roof age, an admitted MGA that "underwrites only resilient roofs" would face a conflict in Texas. It would have to use FORTIFIED/impact-rating *credits* rather than roof-age *exclusions*. Oklahoma HB 2933 died (given), so Oklahoma is currently freer, but that is a political-risk flag.
- Oklahoma HB 3781 (file-and-wait rate review; effective **July 1, 2027**; 30-day review in competitive markets, 60 days in non-competitive ones). **A/B**: https://www.okhouse.gov/posts/news-20260514_1 (search result, not fetched). Effect: slower rate adequacy, which can raise capacity providers' caution on Oklahoma programs (**D**, inference).
- Louisiana Regulation 136 (Apr 29, 2026) mandates FORTIFIED discounts of 16–49% on the hurricane portion, effective Jan 1, 2027 (**B**; search summary of an AOL/LA DOI article).

## 8. The three facts most likely to make or break the thesis
1. **Carrier access and conversion (make/break Phase 1).** Whether independent carriers writing Oklahoma hail risk will appoint a brand-new agency and keep paying 12–15% on volatile premium. Captives (State Farm, Allstate) are unavailable to independents. Openly is the only Oklahoma-active insurtech carrier verified for agents. Unverified, but everything in sec 7a depends on it.
2. **The FORTIFIED book is tiny and hail evidence is missing (breaks Phase 2 as specified).** ~400–500 FORTIFIED roofs in Oklahoma, against a ~$2.56B premium pool. The only strong loss study is for hurricane wind, not hail. Capacity providers will not fund an MGA on a FORTIFIED-only book or on hurricane data. Fix: redefine the underwriting appetite as "verified roof age/material/impact rating" and build hail-specific loss evidence with a carrier partner.
3. **Capacity formation and regulatory drift against selection (makes or breaks Phase 2 timing).** Neptune needed ~7.5 years from 2 to 45 capacity providers. Bamboo depends on one program partner for 84% of premium. Oklahoma loss volatility (2023: 100.8%; Nebraska 2024: 135.7%; Colorado 10-yr UW profit -18.2%) makes profit commissions unreliable. Texas's Aug/Sep 2026 moves (no refusal on roof age) and Oklahoma HB 3781 (July 2027) both squeeze the "select only good roofs / re-price fast" lever.

Runner-up: consumer demand. SOH grant awareness is "low" (KTUL headline, **C**), and a roof costs ~$17.6k, so conversion from "shopper" to FORTIFIED retrofit is a long funnel.
